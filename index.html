03 / GamesMultiplayer in the classroom

Review can be a competition too

Live questions

EduQuiz

The whole class answers the same question from their phones. The projector shows the question, the countdown and the leaderboard; each student sees on their screen whether they got it right and how many points they earned.

  • Points for accuracy, speed and streaks
  • Sound only on the teacher’s computer
  • Questions from your banks
  • Podium and statistics
Cards and patterns

EduCard

A card with a word or picture is called and each student looks for it on their board. The winner is whoever first notices they’ve completed the pattern and claims it: the game doesn’t tell them.

  • How to win, even a drawn shape
  • Picture variant
  • Several forms of the same word
  • Projector and phone at the same time
Real-time memory

EduFlip

Each student has their own board with the same pairs in a different order, and everyone plays at once: nobody waits for a turn. The projector shows the class’s progress without exposing anyone’s mistakes.

  • Points that reward participation
  • Configurable time and extra time
  • Settings in the waiting room
  • Leaderboard and awards

Tap a game to see what it’s about.

Live questions

EduQuiz

The whole class answers the same question from their phones. Each correct answer is worth 500 points plus up to 200 for speed, and streaks give bonus points. The projector shows the question, the countdown, the leaderboard and, at the end, the podium.

Real-time memory

EduFlip

Each student has their own board with the same pairs in a different order, and everyone plays at once. Points come from pairs, speed and streaks; mistakes don’t cost anything. It ends when someone completes their board or time runs out.

Cards and patterns

EduCard

A card with a word or picture is called and each student looks for it on their board. The winner is whoever first completes the chosen pattern (line, corners, L, T, full board or one you draw) and claims it: the game doesn’t tell them.

Spaceship duel

EduDuel

Each student flies a ship in the same arena. A question appears at the top and every ship carries a possible answer: shoot the right one. A wrong hit costs a life, and whoever carries the right answer has to dodge everyone else.

Spot the mistake

EduFix

A sentence appears with a single hidden mistake, and its words move around the screen. Students tap the wrong word: the faster, the more points. Tapping a correct word costs a life.

Question race

EduRace

Everyone answers the same list of questions and moves ahead in the race with each correct answer. A missed question goes back to the end of their list to try again. The first to finish wins.

Lightning sorting

EduSort

A word appears and students tap its category as fast as they can: verb tenses, good and bad practices, whatever you prepare. Mistakes go back to the end of the list. The first to sort everything wins.

Timeline

EduLine

Shuffled cards (historical events, steps of a process, parts of a story) that students drag into order. One attempt per sequence: points for each card in place, and more for a perfect order and speed.

Categories game

EduStop

A letter comes up along with several categories: name, animal, country… Each student writes a word for each one. The first to finish presses “Stop!” and the round ends for everyone. An answer nobody else wrote is worth 10 points; a repeated one, 5.

Word rain

EduRain

Verbs fall and each color marks a verb tense. Students type the correct form before the verb reaches the bottom line; if one gets there, they’re out. Whoever lasts longest wins.

Catch the verb

EduCatch

Verb forms fall and each student catches the one asked for with their basket, dragging it with a finger or tilting the phone. Traps cost lives. It starts with a 3-2-1 countdown and the projector shows a live leaderboard.

Pop the balloon

EduBlast

The top of the screen says which verb forms to find, and students pop the balloons that have them, by tapping the screen or with the camera and the tip of their index finger. Golden balloons bring a surprise question with extra points for whoever solves it first.

Hidden word

EduWord

With a hint, each student guesses their word in six tries. Letters turn green (right place), yellow (somewhere else) or gray (not in the word). Whoever gets it in the fewest tries wins.

05 / SetupYour classroom, your network

Setup guide with your own router

The simplest way is to bring your computer and your own router: students connect to your network and EduOffline works even without internet. We use the Xiaomi Router AX3000T as an example; any router with the same features will do.

1. What you need

MinimumRecommended
Computer64-bit Windows 10 or 11, 4 cores, 8 GB of RAM, SSD with 30 GB free6 or more cores, 16 GB of RAM, 256 GB SSD
RouterDual-band Wi-Fi 5 (802.11ac), for about 25 studentsWi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) with MU-MIMO and OFDMA, like the Xiaomi AX3000T
Students per routerUp to 25 connected at once30 to 40 per router; for more, a second router in mesh
Your computer’s connectionWi-FiCat 5e or Cat 6 network cable (more stable)
PowerAn outlet for the computer and the routerA surge-protected power strip or a small UPS
StudentsAny phone, tablet or computer with an up-to-date browser, with nothing to install

The students-per-router figures are an estimate for real classroom use; manufacturers advertise more devices under lab conditions.

2. What the router needs

  • Dual band (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz). The 5 GHz band is faster and gets congested less.
  • MU-MIMO and OFDMA. They let the router serve several devices at once instead of one by one; OFDMA helps most with Wi-Fi 6 phones.
  • Gigabit ports. To connect your computer by cable.
  • WPA2 or WPA3 security. Use WPA2/WPA3 mixed mode: some older phones won’t connect if you choose WPA3 only.
  • IP address reservation (static DHCP). So your computer always gets the same address.
  • Device-to-device connections allowed. “Client isolation” and guest networks keep students from reaching your computer.
  • Works without internet. It doesn’t need to be connected to a modem.

Example: Xiaomi Router AX3000T

  • Dual-band Wi-Fi 6: up to 2402 Mbps on 5 GHz (with a 160 MHz channel) and 574 Mbps on 2.4 GHz
  • MU-MIMO, OFDMA and WPA3
  • 4 external antennas and 4 Gigabit ports
  • 1.3 GHz dual-core processor and 256 MB of memory
  • Mesh button to add a second router, and NFC pairing with Android phones

3. Set up the router

  1. Plug the router into power. You don’t need to connect it to the internet.
  2. Connect your computer to one of its ports with the network cable (or to its initial Wi-Fi).
  3. Open 192.168.31.1 or router.miwifi.com in your browser. You can also use the Xiaomi app on your phone.
  4. In the setup wizard, create the router’s admin password and write it down.
  5. Name the network, for example Classroom-EduOffline, and set a password for students. Choose WPA2/WPA3 security.
  6. Keep the guest network and any device-isolation option turned off.

4. Give your computer a fixed IP

If your computer’s address changes, students can no longer find it. There are two ways to fix it; we recommend the first.

Option A (recommended): reserve it on the router

  1. Connect your computer to the router the way you’ll use it in class: by cable or by Wi-Fi. Each one has its own physical (MAC) address.
  2. If you connect by Wi-Fi, turn off random addresses for that network in Windows: Settings → Network & internet → Wi-Fi → (your network) → Random hardware addresses: Off. Otherwise the reservation won’t work.
  3. At 192.168.31.1, look for the list of connected devices or the local network (LAN/DHCP) settings and the option to assign a fixed or static IP to a device. The exact name varies with the router’s firmware version.
  4. Choose your computer (by its name or MAC address) and assign it an address, for example 192.168.31.10. Save.
  5. Disconnect and reconnect your computer so it picks up the new address.

Option B: set it in Windows

  1. In the router’s local network settings, check the range of addresses it hands out (DHCP) and pick one outside that range, so no other device gets it.
  2. In Windows: Settings → Network & internet → (Ethernet or Wi-Fi) → IP assignment → Edit → Manual → IPv4.
  3. Enter the chosen address, subnet mask 255.255.255.0, gateway 192.168.31.1 and DNS 192.168.31.1. Save.

If you change routers or networks, set this option back to “Automatic”.

5. Prepare Windows and install EduOffline

  1. Set the router’s network as private: Settings → Network & internet → (your network) → Network profile type: Private. If it stays public, Windows blocks your students.
  2. Install EduOffline and create your teacher account the first time you open it.
  3. From the EduOffline icon next to the clock, choose Copy address for students. It will look something like http://192.168.31.10:3000.

6. Connect your students

  1. Students join the classroom network with the password you created.
  2. They open the address in their browser or scan the QR code on the sign-in screen.
  3. Streaming with your phone’s camera, and the optional gyroscope (EduCatch) and camera (EduBlast) modes, use the secure address (https://…:3001); the first time, accept the browser’s warning.

Linux and macOS

For now the installer is Windows only. On Linux and macOS, EduOffline is installed manually from the program files; it’s meant for users with some terminal experience.

Install and open EduOffline

  1. Install Node.js 20 (LTS version) from nodejs.org.
  2. Open a terminal in the program folder and install its components: npm ci --omit=dev
  3. Start it with your data in a separate folder: EDUOFFLINE_DATA_DIR=~/EduOffline-data node server.js
  4. The first time, the terminal shows the administrator’s temporary password: save it. Then open http://localhost:3000 and create your teacher account.
  5. Leave the terminal open during class; when you close it, EduOffline stops.

On these systems there’s no icon next to the clock and no automatic updates, and real-time captions aren’t available on macOS yet. Some components may ask for build tools when installing.

Address for your students

  • macOS: System Settings → Network → (Wi-Fi or Ethernet) → Details → TCP/IP shows your IP address.
  • Linux: in a terminal, hostname -I.
  • Your students open http://(your address):3000 or scan the QR code on the sign-in screen.

Fixed IP on macOS

  1. To reserve it on the router (option A), turn off the private address for that network: System Settings → Network → Wi-Fi → Details → Private Wi-Fi address: Off.
  2. To set it on the computer (option B): Details → TCP/IP → Configure IPv4: Manually, with the chosen address, subnet mask 255.255.255.0 and router 192.168.31.1. Under DNS add 192.168.31.1.
  3. If the macOS firewall is on, allow incoming connections for node when it asks.

Fixed IP on Linux (Ubuntu and similar)

  1. To reserve it on the router (option A), avoid a random address: in Settings → Wi-Fi → ⚙ for your network → Identity, set the cloned address to Permanent.
  2. To set it on the computer (option B): in ⚙ for your network → IPv4 → Manual, enter the address, subnet mask 255.255.255.0, gateway and DNS 192.168.31.1.
  3. If you use the ufw firewall, open EduOffline’s ports: sudo ufw allow 3000:3001/tcp

If something doesn’t work

  • Students can’t open the page: check that the Windows network is private, that they’re on the same network, and that the router doesn’t isolate devices.
  • It worked and then stopped: your computer’s address probably changed; fix it with step 4.
  • It drops with many students: connect your computer by cable and add a second router in mesh.
  • Exams: a router without internet helps the anti-cheating mode, which pauses the exam if it detects an internet connection.

07 / Frequently asked questionsQuick answers

Frequently asked questions

Do I need internet?

No. EduOffline works on your classroom network, even without internet. Only a few features need it: generating voices for conversations, exporting to Google Forms and downloading updates.

Do my students have to install anything?

No. They sign in from the browser on their phone, tablet or computer, connected to the same network as your computer.

How many students can connect at once?

In our tests, more than 200 students submitted activities at the same time without delays. When a whole class signs in at the same instant, getting in may take a few seconds. In practice, the limit is usually the router: plan on 30 to 40 students per router.

Where is my data stored?

On your computer. On Windows, in %APPDATA%\EduOffline\datos; on Linux and macOS, in the folder you specify when starting it. Updating or uninstalling the program doesn’t delete that folder.

How do I make backups?

In Settings → Automatic backup you choose the folder or USB drive, how many hours between backups and what to include. To restore, pick the backup with Browse and decide whether to merge it with your current data or replace it; both options ask for your password.

Can several teachers use the same computer?

Yes. Each teacher signs in with their own account and sees only their classes, students, content and backups. The administrator creates the teacher accounts and can manage everyone’s information, so that account should belong to someone trustworthy.

Can I use the school’s Wi-Fi?

Yes, if it lets devices communicate with each other. Many school networks block this (client isolation); in that case, use your own router like the one in this guide.

My students can’t open the page. What should I check?

That they’re on the same network as your computer, that the network is set to private in Windows, that the router doesn’t isolate devices, and that your computer hasn’t changed address. To prevent the last one, give it a fixed IP.

My students don’t see the game. Why?

In most games you choose the class in the waiting room before starting, and students only see the game once it has a class. If they still don’t see it, check that they’re enrolled in that class and on the same network as your computer.

Do the games use my students’ camera?

Only EduBlast, and only if the student chooses to: they see themselves on their phone and pop the balloons with their index finger. The image is processed on that phone, without internet, and is never recorded or sent anywhere. The first time, the phone downloads hand tracking (about 10 MB) from your computer, during the waiting room. In EduCatch, the gyroscope works the same way: the phone’s movement only moves the basket. Anyone can always play by tapping the screen.

Why does the browser say the connection isn’t secure?

The secure address (https) uses a certificate created by your own computer, which the browser doesn’t recognize. It’s only needed for streaming with your phone’s camera and for two optional game modes: tilting the phone in EduCatch and playing with the camera in EduBlast. Accept the warning the first time; students who prefer not to can play by tapping the screen.

I forgot my password. What do I do?

If you’re a teacher, the administrator can give you a new one from Accounts. If you forgot the administrator’s, it can be reset from the computer where EduOffline is installed.

How does it update?

On Windows, EduOffline lets you know when there’s a new version. You download it from Settings → Updates and it installs when you close the program or with Install now; a copy of your data is saved first. On Linux and macOS, updating is manual.

What happens if the power goes out?

Whatever was already submitted stays saved. If an exam was running, its time keeps counting even while the computer is off. A small UPS prevents these interruptions.

What can I do from the computer and what from my phone?
Computer

Everything: create classes and units; prepare activities, exams, banks, presentations and lessons; set up grading and download reports; manage accounts, backups and updates.

Phone

Teach while walking around the room: random student, teams, timer, noise traffic light, roll call and QR, announcements, presentations, prepared lessons, games, camera streaming, exam monitoring and bubble-sheet grading, grading submissions and messages. Creating and editing content is done on the computer.

See the phone dashboard in detail

Does it work on Mac and Linux?

Yes, with a manual installation. See the “Linux and macOS” section of the setup guide.
